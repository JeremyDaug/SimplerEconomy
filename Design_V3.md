# Purpose

This is the new design document for our project. Anything written here takes precident over previous design documents. This is meant to be an overarching view of hte design and structure as well as thoughts about how things should operate broadly. This is not meant to be a definition of code, but rather an explanation of intent and design choices.

# Factuals

## Goods

Goods are those things which are traded. They are produced, used, consumed, bought, sold, and destroyed. They are fixed at game start, being considered open to modding.

Goods include a unique ID and name, a class which defines groupings of highly similar goods (currently not used), a decay rate (In * Rate => Out) and decay result (what it becomes upon decaying), a mass and volume (used for Bulk calculations), a list of tags for more complex behavior and rules, and categories, used for searching and organization. A decay rate of 0 means no decay. A rate of 1 means it decays.

### Good Types

These are modifiers and tags.

- Raw Resources, simple, often extracted or refinedfdrom the environment, little to no decay.
- Crafts, common crafts, things anyone could do with a few tools at most.
- Industrial Goods, highly advanced, capital intensive goods that really cannot be easily produced in small businesses.
- Skills, specific skills used to improve the work done in specific jobs.
- Labors, not a specific good, but a kind of good, similar to skills. Used to interface time and skills to more advanced or specialized work, and typically traded from pops to more advanced firms.
- Transportation, can be freely and instantly converted into some amount of Transport at any time (should only do as needed).
- Currency, goods that can be declared as currency and then used. Whether one is used depends on players and market choices. Ordinary goods becoming money is dropped. This is nowhere as dynamic as that was. They can be traded in smaller units (1/1000th of a full unit). The 1/1000 rule might be removed and currency returned to a 1/1 unit, but that would come with additional limitations and explanations.
- Maintenance, a class of goods, similar to labor, but is used to reduce or remove decay from goods like factories.

## Special Goods

- Time, created each morning and spent through the day. Generated by pops mostly. It is one of the most foundational goods and goes into almost everything. Time is special because the system assumes its existence, and thus it cannot be moved around. It must be in, else the system breaks. Excess time is lost at decay, like all other goods. It cannot be used at that point.
- Transport, the unit of moving stuff around. 1 Transport moves 1 person 1 Hex along the map assuming no terrain slows things down. It also moves (TBR) 1 Bulk of goods. Transport is special in that it is the good that pays for friction, so it also needs to be baked in.
- Plots. Land is split between the share, which is the real good, and the plot of land, which is the good. Land shares include more than just the land, as they include the environmental factors and secondary features of the land as well. The plot is updated with alterations (adding or removing forests, fields, orchards, infrastructure, buildings, and so on), which then is exchanged with the share as well.

## Processes

Processes are actions that transform goods into other things.

### Process Types

- Internal, special processes available to anyone/everyone and used to do trivial transformations that have complex inputs or some greater cost. IE, Transportation.
- Agricultural, typically include a plot of land, a starter input (seed grain) or existing capital (orchards), and the right climate.
- Extraction, typically some form of resource extraction from the environment that's not inherently sustainable (lumber, oil extraction, mining, etc) and requires special plots of land to work with.
- Craft, A simple production that is doable as a cottage industry, even if it requires some additional tools or space.
- Industrial, a complex process that cannot be done by hand and typically requires heavy capital to work.
- Maintenance, a process which is allowed to consume used capital and refresh it, dodging it's decay.

#### Special Processes

- Subsistence Process. This changed from a specific process type to a special process. The old mechanism is gone. It is dynamic, producing most basic and common needs for the pop. It takes a sizeable portion of a subsistence pop's time, up to 100%.

# Actors

An Actor is any 'animate' thing that can operate in the game. This is mostly directed to Pops, Firms, Institutions, and Nation. Actors are any entity in the system which can own property and interact with the market.

## Pops

Pops are the lowest kind of actor, representing people themselves. They are the primary source of the ultimate resource, time, and are the end consumer. They are also the laborer and owners.

Pops are divided by location, demographics, craft, and labor. They can be subdivided further to represent specific pops (owners of private firms, political actors, etc).

Pop founding is being simplified and reworked for later, so it is scaled back for the moment.

### Pop Household

A pop's smallest workable unit is the Household, which is divided by age (Child, Adult, Elder) and sex (Male, Female). They are grouped together to form a 'household'. Each category can have a different aspects and be used for different scaling if we so choose. For now though, they are mostly for demographic purposes.

### Pop Property

Pops have 2 kinds of property, goods and shares. 

Goods are the goods from above and represent what they own in that sense. Typically, they should only own those goods they need to satisfy their desires, goods for their jobs (craft inputs, skills, time), and currencies. 

Shares are shares of ownership in other things. At game start, that's the land share, but later on it includes citizenship, and ownership of firms. The land share is the real good for that land. Ownership can be bought and sold to varying degrees based on what is owned and by what mechanism it is being owned.

### Pop Work

Pops have 3 primary factors to them: Subsistence Factor, Craft, and Labor.

Subsistence is a value from 0 to 1 which defines how much of their daily time they put towards the Subsistence Process. Subsistence produces their basic needs and some common needs that they can offer.

Craft is their cottage industry. Small producers who buy their inputs from the market (subsistence might supply some, but only some), and they use their outputs for self-satisfaction and sell the rest.

Labor is their wage job, which they can offer on the market just like their craft, but it has no inputs beyond skill and time. It's treated separately from Craft as it distinguishes from a cottage style worker and a member of the labor force.

A pop can have one of each, with it's time divided between them in simple allocation mechanisms. Pops don't deal with complexity. They just have their 3 options. Everything else is secondary.

### Demographics

- Species, the biological baseline of the pop. Generally highly fixed, but can be modified in small ways. Assimilation can only occur on this metric by special means.
  - Ethnicity, the biological variance from the baseline of the pop. Assimilation can occur, but does so in line with birthrates. Slow and very difficult to change, most changes are random.
- Culture, the cultural identity of the demographic. Starts as 'everything above biology', but can diverge later with development and institutions.
  - Subculture, what it says on the tin. It's a modification of the original culture, creating a new subculture. These cover everything from Class, to Caste.
- Religion, the spiritual identity of the demographic. Tied to a faith and easier to transmit or convert than culture.
- Ideology, the 'highest' level of identity. Tied in heavily with mass politics.

Only Species and Culture will be worked on at the moment. All others can come later as they should be mostly based on those. Stratum is renamed Subculture. Subculture and Religion are on the back burner.

### Desires

Pops needs are called desires and come in a 3 tier set. Basic, Common, and Luxury. Basic desires are those needed to keep the pop alive. If they have none of them, they are staving and will begin dying shortly thereafter. Common desires are those things that are considerde 'necissary' for a respectable life for that pop. Luxury needs are those which a pop wants and can always have more of.

Desires have a bucket of goods they seek out to satisfy them and include an efficiency factor for it. The exact amount of satisfaciton needed can scale with any part or division of the household.

Desires have a list of goods in them called a bucket which attaches an efficiency rating to each and defines how units convert into satisfaction on a per-unit basis. 

What goods are allowed in this bucket are defined by the Platonic Desire, which defines how other desires in a game are allowed to look. They are converted into Demographic desires which are a attached to demographics and define what the that demographic is working with specifically. That is then converted down into a Desire, which is attached to a pop and is scaled correctly to match that pop.

By default, every good in a bucket has a minimum requirement of `1 / 2 * Goods in the bucket`. A pop is not required to hit that minimum, but it must try to satsify that to the best of their ability. Desires may have goods added or removed by the player, but a desire must always have at least 1 good. If a player wants to swap out the last good of a desire, there will be an intermediary period where both goods are allowed at the same time as the pop moves over. That transition is allowed to take (TBR) 10 turns.

Desires can also update at day start, if a new one is added or its bucket edited, but that doesn't change size in most cases, and desire planning is fairly ad hoc as it is, buying and reserving mostly as it goes.

## Firms

A firm is a special entity which acts as a focal point of production distinct from a pop. They are almost purely economic actors and are mostly independent from the players in terms of their logic and actions.

Firms buy goods for their inputs and sell their outputs. Their outputs can feed into other processes they do, but additional effects like this come at a cost of complexity.

### Firm Types

- Producers, focus on a set of processes, typically with an emphasis on a small set of goods they wish to make, and focus on making that good to the exclusion of everything else.
- Merchants, focus on storage, maintenance, and arbitrage of common trade goods, especially arbitrage between currencies in the local market. 
- Traders, focus on transporting goods between markets, gaining arbitrage in that fashion.

The wider structure will be brought back. These three are just the summary of logical branches for firms.

Deals should no longer be necessary. Contracts are likely to go away, but could come back as a special firm-firm trade action.

## Institutions

Institutions are organizations that operate semi-independently from the players. They have a structure that paralells the Firm, and operate for their own goals and reasons, but if kept happy, will make you stronger and give bonuses.

## Units

Units are far down the road. They represent military units, traders, worker armies, and the like. They won't be brought in for a while, so being undeclared for now is fine.

## Nation

Nations are the Players of the game (both human and Computer) and represent a people, their governments, and institutions. While it will be treated as effectively Synonymous with a State or Government, it includes more abstract or immaterial things like the culture, religion, and institutions. The player is effectively the "Spirit of the Nation", having influence over the lands, peoples, institutions, and governments of these nations.

### Nation Resources

Abstract resources available to nations and is the primary 'action' currency of the game.

- Unity,
- Development,
- Legitimacy,
- Authority,
- Faith,

Culture and Research have been renamed to Unity and Development. Development effectively combines the purposes of Culture and Research. Unity is the maintenance resource for keeping a nation together.

# Market and Map

## Map

The map is a hexgrid with edgewrapping, similar to Civilization 5-6 and Millenia. Each Hex is also known as a Tile, and every tile has it's own climate data and it's own populations.

## Tiles

Tiles are individual hexes on the map, in includes fixed environmental data at the start, and is used to define what is where.

## Regions and Markets

Regions and Markets are Synonymous logically, but slightly different in code representation, with Regions representing the tiles on the map, and the Market representing the people and resources collected into the region. 

Markets are effectively the smallest market unit in the game. It is where actors trade between each other, and it is the primary manager of local economies. Firms, pops, and the like have hex locations which should be contained in the market and 

Markets connect to other regions for trade.

Claims and war are still intended. They are not written up here yet. 

### How the Market works

The market includes a list of prices in the various currencies of the world. All goods get a trade rate, with currencies being allowed to divide by 1/1000 instead of 1 whole unit. That 1/1000 rule might return to 1/1.

The actors of the market are the sellers, and they sell whatever goods they produce. Pops sell from their subsistence, craft, and labor. Firms sell their outputs.

When an actor goes shopping, they go with a specific good/goal in mind, defined by their needed inputs, desires, and plans. Each actor has their own plan and acts on that plan. There is a priority between actors, but for the most part the actor who goes first is the wealthiest per household. Early Player, Institutions, Firms, Pops, Late Player is the rough phase order. Players can buy either first or last. Institutions mostly act through firms, so if something is actually operating as an institution it always goes first. Pops buying for a desire select the good that has the lowest cost per satisfaction (unit defined by the primary (most used) currency of the market currently). For inputs to processes, they go for required goods first, then optional goods, with optional goods being excludable if including them would make the process less profitable. For traders and merchants, they look for goods that are profitable to store and maintain (maintenance good cheaper than losses of storage), and arbitrage opportunities.

The unit of account, is either the good with the highest durability, or the declared unit of account.

Sellers try to balance production, storage, decay, and selling. They want to maximize production, minimize decay, maximize selling, and keep storage balanced.

Prices are static throughout the day, to keep the immediate buying and selling simple. They are updated at day end after a market stops trading. They move based on surplus and shortage, plus a default movement when no trade occurs that moves the price to the least efficient production method's cost + 10%, to help create production for it without demanding an actor lose from day 1. These prices move per currency, not as a block, so prices in specific currencies can diverge, and that divergence can be taken advantage of.

The surplus and shortage movement follows $price_{t+1,C}=price_{t,C} \times ( 1 + k \times clamp(\frac{demand in C - supply in C}{max(demand in C, supply in C)}, c, -c))$ where $C$ is the units of currency traded, $k$ is gain/loss constant (0.05-0.15), and c is our movement caps (e.g. 20%).

# Turn Order

Turn order is: (TBR)

The old market day consolidated a whole turn into the market. That was not the intent. A turn is interleaved with other markets and external effects. The ordering was roughly correct.

1. Start of day (time generation). Desires can also update here, if a new one is added or its bucket edited. Special resource extraction can be here, depending on what kind of special.
2. Environmental phase. Currently not much. This is where disasters or climactic shifts would occur.
3. Player Action Phase (map actions, unit movements, etc)
4. Apply player demographic changes and bonuses. Bonuses are collected earlier in the day and dispensed of where needed. Pop change bonuses go to growth. Satisfaction and sentiments go to sentiments.
5. Actor Reservations. Everyone gets a chance, before the market and production, to lock away goods they don't want to sell. This is mostly for preserving internal stockpiles that could either be sold or used as inputs or consumption.
6. Intra Market Day. Each actor acts on their own plan.
7. Inter Market Day. Trade between markets. This focuses mostly on setting up trade routes, creating shipping plans, and then moving them between markets to buy and sell.
8. Production. Special resource extraction can be here, depending on what kind of special.
9. Consumption. Pops consume for their desires.
10. Pop Sentiments
11. Growth. Births and deaths, rebalance if capped, prune pops too small to survive, and migration. Local migration is the same hex. Regional migration is between hexes. Interregional migration is between regions and markets, and is more involved. Internal migration offer math is likely going to be similar to the previous math. The cap is mostly a testing mechanism. It could be made a tool of the player later, but that is hypothetical.
12. Decay. Goods and desires. Excess time is lost here, like all other goods. It cannot be used at that point. Desire decay uses the same meaning as good decay.
13. Rescale. Processes, plans, and desires (mostly satisfaction), mostly for pops. Whenever a pop grows, its production and consumption doesn't immediately match, so most of the matching is done here.
14. Record keeping
15. Planning

Player and actor changes to the environment would possibly need a phase near the end, before planning, so they can be taken into account smoothly.
